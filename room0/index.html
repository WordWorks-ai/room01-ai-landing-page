room0 is the authority substrate underneath your client app. Your app owns conversation and user experience; room0 owns policy enforcement, tool boundaries, and execution receipts.

What room0 owns

room0 is intentionally narrow: it governs capability access and execution integrity, while client apps stay focused on product experience.

Policy engine

Decides ask / allow / block with context-aware rules, capability scopes, and explicit boundary checks.

Tool gateway

Mediates connector calls behind scoped credentials so agents cannot bypass policy or overreach connector permissions.

Receipts and audit trail

Every proposed, approved, denied, and executed action leaves structured evidence your app can display and operators can verify.

Versioned contracts

Stable interfaces between app, agent runtime, and platform services so teams can move quickly without integration drift.

Least-privilege defaults

Connector boundaries are explicit: read vs write, draft vs send, propose vs execute.

Fail-closed posture

If policy cannot be evaluated or provenance cannot be recorded, the action does not execute.

How a client app integrates with room0

room0 sits between user intent and real-world tool execution, keeping the safety boundary consistent across all channels.

1
The app captures intent

Your client experience receives a user request and translates it into a proposed action.

2
room0 evaluates policy

room0 checks identity, context, scope, and risk posture before granting any capability.

3
Tool access is brokered

Allowed actions run through the gateway with bounded credentials and connector-specific controls.

4
Receipts flow back to the app

Your client can show users what happened, why it was allowed, and what was blocked.
